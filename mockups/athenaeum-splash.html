AI does the archiving.
You do the science.

Athenaeum is a research traceability tool for working scientists — built on the [hopefully non-controversial] belief that every claim, human- or computer-generated, must be anchored to specific sentences, in specific papers, verified.

The manifesto

Reject synthesis
embrace complexity.

01 · The problem
The tools are pulling in the wrong direction
Scientists spend 3–8 hours a week reading literature. Every major AI tool has optimized for the same paradigm: ask a question, get a synthesized answer. The design goal is speed-to-answer — which is fundamentally mismatched to how science actually works.
consensus meters reduce evidence to vote counts
02 · The failure
Hallucinated citations are polluting the literature
Closed, non-reproducible tools optimized for consumer-scale growth are introducing misread and fabricated citations into active scientific discourse. Real research is a sustained engagement over months and years — not a sequence of stateless questions.
closed · non-reproducible · unaccountable
03 · The principle
Every claim anchored to a specific sentence
Every AI-generated connection is anchored to specific sentences in specific papers, quoted verbatim, verified by an independent checking pass. All reasoning is auditable. You no longer trust that a summary has successfully digested citations X, Y, and Z — you see the textual network directly.
verbatim · sentence-level · independently verified
04 · The architecture
Annotations and questions are first-class objects
Annotations, open questions, and discussions accumulate into a personal knowledge graph. When made public, they form a community corpus that enables users to build consensus and independently trace and audit evidence bases. AI makes connections. The scientist evaluates them in a structured citation, annotation, connection, cementation activity loop.
knowledge graph · community corpus · open audit
05 · The infrastructure
Built on open science
OpenAlex indexes 271 million works under CC0. Preprint servers host millions of full texts. The infrastructure to do better has quietly become available. What was missing was a product built on this foundation that treats scientists as scientists — not passive consumers of AI output.
OpenAlex · CC0 · open annotation standards
06 · The commitment
Open source, POSI-committed, nonprofit governed
Our commitment to POSI (Principles of Open Scholarly Infrastructure) and to nonprofit governance is structural, not rhetorical — the legal form prevents the main failure mode of successful open infrastructure: acquisition by commercial publishers.
POSI · open prompts · open evaluations · CC-BY
07 · The philosophy
AI is the archivist who searches the library.
The user is the scholar who interprets.

The future world crafted by scientists and engineers is ultimately inhabited by humans, not computers. Humans need to be responsible for assessing claims — not abstracting away intellectual engagement in favor of expediency.

AI is powerful for assessing large volumes of text and presenting coherent structure. This tool uses that power honestly: AI surfaces connections, flags tensions, and traces evidence chains. You decide what it means.

How it works

Structured around sustained
research — not one-off queries.

01
Scope a project
Create a project based on an active research question. The tool learns and searches the literature specific to your work — not a generic knowledge base. Papers, datasets, and prior knowledge bricks accumulate into a persistent and human-readable research context.
02
Synthesize
Evaluate connections that AI surfaces. Evaluate the evidence yourself, drawing upon the direct textual evidence.
03
Cement
Citations, evidence, and annotations build knowledge bricks. Cement these into human-readable sentences that build a knowledge base.
04
Trace, export, and share
The full knowledge graph — every claim, every supporting passage, every open question and tension — is exportable as a portable .ath framework file. Human-readable, version-controllable, and reproducible.
05
Repeat
Each cycle deepens the knowledge base. The context accumulates; the literature narrows; the claims sharpen.
Open infrastructure
The reform-minded scientific community will not trust closed commercial AI for serious work.

The market has consolidated around a single product paradigm that experienced researchers find unsatisfying. No major tool is open source. Foundation funding for open scientific infrastructure is at a historic high — following cOAlition S, the U.S. OSTP Nelson memo, and European institutional mandates.

We publish our citation verification rate monthly and openly. We target a <2% hallucinated-citation rate — measured, not claimed. Our prompts and evaluation logic are public. If we fail, anyone can see it.
