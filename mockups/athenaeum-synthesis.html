Fungal dominance is theorized to yield higher CUE because fungi express a broader enzyme suite and carry a greater biomass C:N ratio.
"A shift toward a fungal dominance in the microbial community is thought to enhance organic C accumulation and decrease its turnover rate… fungi are thought to express a broader suite of enzymes capable of transforming and stabilizing inputs; and fungal biomass has greater C:N ratio which results in increased carbon use efficiency."
¶ Containing paragraph · §Introduction, paragraph 3
Intensively managed farmed soils often exhibit lower F:B biomass ratios when compared with more extensively managed soils; a phenomena thought to be due to tillage, high rates of fertilization and decreasing C:N ratio favoring bacteria (Bardgett et al., 1996; Bailey et al., 2002; Sinsabaugh et al., 2013). Lower fungal biomass has been linked to lower capacity of such soils to sequester C. A shift toward a fungal dominance in the microbial community is thought to enhance organic C accumulation and decrease its turnover rate due to enhanced fungal mediated soil aggregation and/or changes in the physiology of the microbial biomass (Six et al., 2006). For instance, fungi are thought to express a broader suite of enzymes capable of transforming and stabilizing inputs; and fungal biomass has greater C:N ratio which results in increased carbon use efficiency (Strickland and Rousk, 2010; Waring et al., 2013).
Malik et al. 2016 · Frontiers in Microbiology 7: 1247

Community composition (of which F:B ratio is one dimension) is the strongest biotic predictor of CUE in controlled model soils — though abiotic factors modulate the relationship.
"community composition and diversity are the strongest predictors of CUE, abiotic factors modulated the relationship between diversity and CUE, with CUE being positively correlated with bacterial diversity only under high moisture."
Domeignoz-Horta et al. 2020 · Nature Communications 11: 3684

Direct empirical measurement across nine field sites finds the opposite sign: higher fungal dominance correlates with lower CUE, not higher.
"We observed that fungal-to-bacterial growth ratios (F:B) ranged from 0.02 to 0.44… We found a negative exponential relationship between the dominance of fungi and the microbial CUE. CUE ranged from 0.03 to 0.30, and values clustered most strongly according to site rather than level of soil N."
Soares & Rousk 2019 · Soil Biology and Biochemistry 131: 195–205
